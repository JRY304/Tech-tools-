## 1. Bottom line

**Belgium is genuinely unoccupied on both sides.** Neither Sri Sri Tattva Europe nor AMLA Natur has anyone responsible for Belgium, any Belgian stockist, or a French-language shop. That is a real opening, and it is why they want freelancers.

**Your read of their strategy is probably right, and it is the core risk.** They want freelancers to do the expensive part (finding and opening accounts) and will take over the relationships once these are running. Your protection is the contract, not their goodwill: commercial-agent status, commission on repeat orders and on all Belgian sales for the life of the contract, a tail after it ends, and Belgian law. Without those, you are building their network for free.

**The multi-brand "bol.com of Ayurveda" is not viable**; a small curated Belgian shop is, but only as a side business. About 25 online shops already reach Belgian buyers, and Sri Sri Tattva Europe itself already sells on bol.com and Amazon.com.be. The real gap is narrow: no sizeable Belgian, bilingual NL/FR, advice-led Ayurveda shop. That supports a lifestyle business, not a platform, and carrying competing brands will likely breach a Sri Sri Tattva agency contract.

**Recommendation:** go for the agency, not the webshop, as the main play. Negotiate it as a combined AMLA plus Sri Sri Tattva mandate for Belgium (ideally Benelux), start with the 10 to 15 strongest leads in practitioners and independents to prove sell-through, and treat any webshop as a later, negotiated add-on (localised storefront or marketplace operation on their behalf, with a buy-out formula), not as a competitor to their own shop.

## 3. Contract mechanics, answered

**Commercial agent.** A protected legal status (Belgian Code of Economic Law, Book X) for an independent with a standing mandate to find and negotiate deals for a principal, paid by commission. It brings mandatory notice (1 month per started year, max 6), a goodwill indemnity of up to one year's average commission, and a right to see the figures. The alternative they will likely propose, a business introducer (*apporteur d'affaires*), gets none of that. A BV can be a commercial agent, so BV to BV is fine.

**Which of their entities signs.** Not automatically "their BV". There are at least three candidates: Sri Sri Tattva Europe B.V. (NL), Sri Sri Tattva (Germany) GmbH, and AMLA Natur Vertriebs GmbH. Contract with the entity that invoices Belgian customers, or with all of them, otherwise you cannot verify your commission. Ask for a parent-company guarantee: the B.V. has EUR 10k capital.

**Commission on their web orders.** You do not get this automatically. EU and Belgian law entitle an agent with an *exclusive* territory to commission on sales to customers in that territory even without the agent's involvement, but this is generally treated as excludable by contract, and they will try to exclude their own webshop. Write it in explicitly, typically at a lower rate (3 to 5%) than deals you close (10 to 12%).

**How you check it.** The right to a commission statement at least quarterly, and to extracts from their books to verify it, is mandatory and cannot be contracted away. In practice: a monthly order export from their shop (Shopify) filtered on shipping country Belgium, plus their B2B invoice list for Belgian customers. Five minutes of work for them; a refusal is a signal.

**Retainer.** For a part-time market opener, EUR 1,000 to 2,500 a month for 6 to 12 months is a reasonable ask. Alternatives: an advance deducted from later commission (a "draw"), or them paying samples, fair stands, travel and NL/FR materials. Expect refusal of cash given how they operate; then push for 15% in year 1 and full cost coverage.

**Tail payments.** Separate from the statutory indemnity: commission on repeat orders from customers you brought in, for 2 to 3 years after the contract ends. Make it explicitly *on top of* the indemnity.

**Sellable exit.** Only exists if you own something: a webshop, stock, customer contracts in your name. As an agent your exit is the indemnity plus the tail. If you want a buy-out later, it has to be a distributor or webshop-operator structure with the price formula (e.g. 2 to 3x the last 12 months' profit) written in from day one.

**Non-compete.** Expect a clause forbidding competing brands during the contract (lawful) and possibly after (Belgian law caps a post-contract non-compete for agents at 6 months and restricts it to the same territory and goods). This directly limits the multi-brand shop idea.

## 4. What you would actually do: market-opening playbook

The job has three parts: **open** accounts, **service** them (reorders, shelf, staff training, promotions, complaints), and **report** back. Opening is what they say they want; servicing is the part that creates recurring value and the part they will want to take over. Your commission must follow the account, not the first order.

### Phase 0: before signing (month 0 to 1)

- Contract terms per section 3.
- Get the **sellable range for Belgium in writing**: which SKUs are EU-compliant, have NL/FR labels, and (for supplements) have been notified to FPS Public Health via FOODSUP. Without this you sell nothing. Ashwagandha is reportedly not allowed in Belgian supplements.
- Get the **trade terms**: wholesale price list, retailer margin (expect 40 to 50% needed), minimum order (EUR 150 to 250 for small shops), delivery time to Belgium, who handles orders and invoices, returns policy.
- Get a **sample and materials budget**: sample kits, NL/FR product sheets, a price list in EUR, a B2B order form or portal.

### Phase 1: proof of sell-through (months 1 to 6)

Small, reachable outlets that decide on the spot. The goal is not revenue; it is evidence (reorders, sell-through numbers, testimonials) to take to wholesalers and chains.

## 5. The online side

**Who runs it.** Sri Sri Tattva Europe B.V. runs srisritattva.eu as a single Shopify store for all of Europe, shipping from (likely) Waalwijk. Nobody is assigned to Belgium. The B.V. also sells on bol.com and Amazon.com.be. AMLA runs its own DE/EN shops and does not localise for Belgium either.

**Three ways you could be involved online, ranked:**

1. **Commission on Belgian web orders** (section 3). No work, no risk, pure upside. Ask for it first.
2. **Operate the Benelux/France online channel for them as a service:** French storefront, NL/FR content, bol.com and Amazon BE/FR listings, customer service. Paid as a fee plus revenue share, with a buy-out formula. This fills their actual gap (no French, no localisation, sold-out stock) and does not compete with them. Capital-light. Risk: no legal protection unless contracted.
3. **Your own multi-brand Ayurveda shop for Belgium.** See below. Weakest of the three.

**The multi-brand shop, tested:**

- **Marketplace ("bol.com of Ayurveda")**: not feasible. A marketplace needs many sellers and many buyers at the same time; brands already reach Belgian buyers through bol.com, Amazon and 25 specialist shops.
- **Competition:** Belgian specialists (Ananda Ayurveda, Mala India, Herboristerie Sanjivani, Art of Vedas), Dutch specialists shipping to Belgium (Pranayur largest at about 332 Trustpilot reviews, Holisan, De Ayurveda Specialist, Surya, Maharishi Ayurveda NL), the Austrian niceshops group (Ayurveda101, VitalAbo, Ecco Verde, which already carry Classic Ayurveda), generalists (Holland & Barrett, Newpharma, Kazidomi, bol.com).
- **The gap:** no sizeable Belgian-owned, bilingual NL/FR, advice-led Ayurveda shop. Small review counts across the board suggest a niche market.
- **Feasible model:** curated hybrid. Stock 3 to 6 fast movers, dropship the long tail, sell B2B to the same practitioners and studios. Unit economics from V1 apply: about EUR 9.75 left per EUR 40 order before marketing, paid acquisition EUR 20 to 40, so it only works on repeat customers and community channels.
- **Conflict:** carrying Maharishi, Himalaya or others while holding a Sri Sri Tattva/AMLA mandate will likely breach the non-compete. Note that AMLA itself already sells competing brands, so negotiate the right to sell **AMLA's existing third-party range** too: that gives you multi-brand breadth with one supplier and no conflict.

13. Wholesale terms, commission rates, retainer range and margins are industry rules of thumb, not sourced data.
14. Lead list: about 20 rows link to directories instead of the business's own site, 6 phone numbers from directories, several possibly outdated. Verify before contact.
15. Commission on web orders being excludable by contract, and the 6-month cap on agents' post-contract non-compete, are from general knowledge of Belgian law. Confirm with a lawyer.

**Concerns about the model:**
16. Their likely plan (freelancers open, company takes over) is rational for them and only fair to you with commission on repeat orders, a tail and the indemnity.
17. Post-acquisition integration: the people you negotiate with now may not be in charge in 6 months. Get everything in writing, signed by a director of the contracting entity.
18. A Belgian commercial agency/distribution lawyer should review the contract before signing. This report is not legal advice.
