## Outreach playbook

### 1. Sequence (per lead, about 3 weeks)

| Step | Day | Action | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | D0 | Short intro email (NL or FR) to the generic business address (info@, contact@) | 120 words max. One line on each brand, one line on why them (cite their treatment/shop), offer a free sample kit and a 15-minute visit. PDF one-pager attached, no price list yet. Include opt-out line. |
| 2 | D3 to D5 | Phone call | Ask who decides on purchasing, confirm interest in a sample drop. Practitioners often answer only between sessions: call at 9:00 or after 18:00. |
| 3 | D7 to D14 | Visit with samples | Book a slot; do not walk in on yoga studios during class or on shops on Saturday. Cluster visits by city (Antwerpen, Gent, Brussel, Liege/Namur, Leuven/Mechelen days). |
| 4 | Visit +7 | Follow-up email | Recap, price list, opening-order offer, order form. |
| 5 | Visit +21 | Second follow-up call | Feedback on samples; propose a small opening order or a consignment trial. |
| 6 | Stop | If no reply after step 5, park lead for 6 months | Log the contact and the opt-out status in your CRM. |

**Order of attack:** (1) Ayurveda practitioners and schools (they explain the product to end customers and use oils in treatments), (2) Art of Living network (Sri Sri Tattva is the founder's own brand: warmest audience), (3) Indian grocers in Brussels for Sri Sri Tattva food and personal care, (4) independent health stores with supplement shelves, (5) wholesalers once you have 10 to 20 active accounts as proof of sell-through, (6) chains (Farm, Bio-Planet, Ekoplaza) last.

**Channel split:** practitioners and yoga studios can be served direct. Organic shops in Belgium mostly buy through Biofresh or Vajra-Delibio and prefer not to open a new supplier account for a few SKUs; so for segment D, plan from the start to land one wholesaler (Mannavita or Biofresh) or use Ankorstore for small accounts.

### 2. Sample kit and material

- **Practitioner kit (segment A):** AMLA Natur massage oil(s) in a treatment size plus a retail size, one Classic Ayurveda/AyuCare churna or tea, Sri Sri Tattva Chyawanprash or Triphala, Sri Sri Tattva tulsi/ayurvedic tea. Bring a tester they can use in one treatment.
- **Retail kit (segments C and D):** 4 to 6 fastest-moving SKUs per brand in retail packaging, a shelf-talker or small counter display, and product cards in NL and FR.
- **Paperwork:** one-page brand sheet per brand in NL and FR; price list (wholesale and RRP incl. 6% or 21% VAT as applicable); ingredient lists and organic certificates (EU organic logo, certifier code); for food supplements, proof that each product is notified to the FPS Public Health (Belgian notification number, NUT/PL number) and labels in NL, FR and DE as Belgian labelling requires (verify with the brand owners); cosmetics: CPNP notification and EU Responsible Person.
- **Claims:** do not make medical claims in emails or on shelf cards. EU Regulation 1924/2006 limits health claims; botanical claims are "on hold" and must be worded carefully. Have the brands supply approved wording.
- **For Art of Living:** event packs (small sizes for course participants) and a commission or fundraising margin for the centre if they host a sales table.

### 3. Typical wholesale terms for small retailers and practitioners (starting proposal, adjust to brand pricing)

- **Retailer margin:** 40 to 50% of the retail price excl. VAT (i.e. wholesale = RRP excl. VAT x 0.50 to 0.60). Belgian organic shops expect about 35 to 45% on products bought via a wholesaler; buying direct, ask for the top of the range. Practitioners using products in treatments: professional price 10 to 15% below shop wholesale on treatment sizes.
- **Minimum opening order:** EUR 150 to 250 excl. VAT for shops, EUR 100 for practitioners; repeat orders EUR 100. Free delivery above about EUR 250 (Belgian wholesalers call this the "franco" threshold).
- **Payment:** 30 days end of invoice for established shops; pro forma or payment on delivery for first order with very small accounts.
- **Risk reducers for a new brand:** sale-or-return (consignment) only for the opening order and only for a limited time (for example 90 days) on unopened stock; or a "guaranteed sale" swap of slow movers. Offer one free tester per SKU and 1 free unit per 12 on opening orders (a "12 + 1" deal is common in the Belgian health trade).
- **Wholesaler terms (Biofresh, Vajra-Delibio, Mannavita):** distributor margins are typically 25 to 35% on top of the brand's ex-works price, plus listing fees or promo support in some cases. Get these in writing from them; figures above are market rules of thumb, not sourced from these companies.

### 4. GDPR and e-mail rules for B2B cold outreach in Belgium

- **Two layers apply:** (a) the GDPR (lawful basis for processing a person's data) and (b) the e-privacy rules in the Code of Economic Law, Book XII, art. XII.13 plus the Royal Decree of 4 April 2003 (rules for unsolicited email advertising).
- **Book XII starting point:** email advertising needs prior consent (art. XII.13). The Royal Decree of 4 April 2003 makes an exception for email addresses of legal persons (companies, vzw/asbl) that are impersonal, e.g. info@, contact@, sales@. You may email those without prior consent, as long as you offer a free, easy opt-out in every message and identify yourself.
- **Named addresses are not covered:** firstname@company.be is a personal address. The FPS Economy guidance cited in secondary sources says you need consent for these. Many of your leads (sole-trader practitioners, yoga teachers) are natural persons, not legal persons: even their info@ address falls outside the legal-person exception. For them, a single, personalised, clearly relevant business proposal is the defensible practice, but the strict reading of Book XII says consent is needed. Lowest-risk route for sole traders: phone first or use their contact form, and email only after they agree.
- **GDPR basis:** legitimate interest (art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) can cover B2B prospecting, with a documented balancing test, data minimisation (only business contact data from their public site), an information notice in the first message (who you are, where you got the address, right to object), and immediate handling of objections. The Belgian DPA (Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit / Autorite de protection des donnees) set out its direct marketing position in Recommendation 01/2020 (17 January 2020); secondary sources mention a newer consultation draft (01/2025). Read the current version on gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit.be before scaling.
- **Practical checklist:** use generic company addresses where they exist; one email plus at most two follow-ups; every email contains your full identity, VAT number and an opt-out line ("Reply 'stop' and I will not contact you again"); keep a suppression list; never buy email lists; keep a simple record of where each address came from (the Website column in this file).
- **Enforcement:** Book XII is enforced by the FPS Economy Economic Inspection (criminal fines possible); the GDPR by the Belgian DPA.

### 5. Language etiquette

- **Flanders (Antwerpen, Gent, Leuven, Brugge, Mechelen, Hasselt, Kortrijk):** write and speak Dutch. Starting in French is a faux pas; English is the acceptable fallback if your Dutch is weak, not French.
- **Wallonia (Liege, Namur, Waterloo):** French only. Use "vous" until invited otherwise.
- **Brussels:** bilingual by law; most businesses operate in French, many Flemish-run shops in Dutch. Check the language of their website and mirror it. If unclear, send a short bilingual email (FR first, NL below) or ask on the phone. Many yoga studios and Indian grocers work in English.
- **Materials:** all printed material and product cards in both NL and FR; product labels must also comply with Belgian language rules (NL, FR and DE for food).
- **Tone:** Belgians value modesty and directness; avoid hard-sell. Practitioners respond to knowledge (ingredients, traditional use, sourcing), shops to rotation and margin.
