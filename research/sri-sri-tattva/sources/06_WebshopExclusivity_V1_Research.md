## 1. EU competition law: what exclusivity can legally mean

### 1.1 Framework
- Vertical Block Exemption Regulation (EU) 2022/720 (VBER), in force 1 June 2022 to 31 May 2034, plus Commission Vertical Guidelines 2022/C 248/01. Safe harbour applies if supplier and buyer each have <= 30% market share on their relevant markets (supplier: Ayurvedic personal care in BE/EU will be far below 30%, so the VBER applies). https://www.luther-lawfirm.com/en/newsroom/blog/detail/brand-new-block-exemption-regulation-for-distribution-agreements-vber-changes-effective-from-1-june-2022-new-flexibility-and-and-pitfalls-for-companies , https://www.linklaters.com/knowledge/publications/alerts-newsletters-and-guides/2022/may/13/vertical-block-exemption-regulation-final-text-published-and-ready-for-launch-on-1-june-2022
- Below 30% share and no hardcore restrictions = clauses are exempt. With a hardcore clause, the whole agreement loses the safe harbour and the clause is void.

### 1.2 What the brand CAN promise you (lawful exclusivity)
- **Exclusive territory (Art. 4(b)(i) VBER):** the brand can appoint you exclusive distributor for Belgium (or Benelux), i.e. promise not to appoint other distributors there, and require all its OTHER distributors (in NL, DE, FR...) not to ACTIVELY sell into Belgium. Since 2022 a territory can be shared by up to 5 exclusive distributors ("shared exclusivity"). The brand can also pass the active-sales ban on to the other distributors' direct customers.
- **Active-sales restrictions online are allowed:** e.g. other distributors can be barred from Belgian-targeted online ads, a .be domain, NL/FR language versions targeted at BE, Belgian price-comparison targeting, paid search on Belgian geo. (Guidelines treat territory-targeted online advertising as active selling.) https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2022/05/new-vber-and-guidelines-provide-clarification-for-online-sales-restrictions
- **Marketplace bans:** under the 2022 Guidelines a ban on selling via third-party marketplaces (Amazon, bol) is NOT a hardcore restriction, irrespective of product category (codifies Coty C-230/16, which concerned luxury cosmetics). So the brand may lawfully forbid ALL its distributors from Amazon/bol, which would protect your own webshop from intra-brand price wars on marketplaces. Caveat: this binds only the brand's contractual partners, not independent parallel traders who bought stock elsewhere (e.g. imported Indian stock). https://www.mondaq.com/uk/antitrust-eu-competition/1194686/new-vber-and-guidelines-provide-clarification-for-online-sales-restrictions , https://www.lexgo.be/fr/actualites-et-articles/4877-the-court-of-justice-of-the-eu-nds-that-luxury-goods-suppliers-can-prevent-authorized-distributors-from-selling-such-goods-on-third-party-platforms-judgment-of-7-december-2017-in-case-c-230-16-coty-germany-gmbh-v-parfumerie-akzente-gmbh
- **Dual pricing** (different wholesale price for online vs offline) is no longer hardcore (Art. 4(e) narrowed), so the brand may price differently to online sellers.
- **Non-compete** (you only sell Sri Sri Tattva Ayurveda, not competing brands like Himalaya/Dabur/Kerala Ayurveda): allowed up to 5 years per term (tacit renewal now allowed if you can renegotiate/exit). Something the brand will likely ask for in exchange for exclusivity.

### 1.3 What the brand CANNOT promise you (hardcore, void)
- **Passive sales ban:** the brand cannot stop a Dutch, German or French distributor (or the brand's own EU webshop) from serving a Belgian customer who finds them on their own (Google organic, word of mouth). A Belgian visitor ordering from srisritattva.de must be served.
- **Geo-blocking Regulation (EU) 2018/302, Art. 6:** any clause requiring a trader not to respond to unsolicited demand from consumers in other Member States is automatically void, regardless of market share (stricter than VBER). Distributors cannot be forced to block Belgian IPs, auto-redirect, or refuse Belgian addresses (they can however refuse to SHIP to BE if they do not ship there at all, and offer pickup in their own country under the same conditions). https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2018/global/new-eu-geoblocking-regulation-what-businesses-selling-into-the-eu-need-to-do-to-comply , https://lxgesetze.de/geoblocking-vo/6
- **Banning online sales** or the effective use of the internet (Art. 4(e)) is hardcore. You cannot be banned from selling online; but equally the brand cannot ban OTHER distributors from having a website accessible to Belgians.
- **Resale price maintenance:** the brand cannot fix your retail price (recommended / maximum prices OK). Nor can you get the brand to fix other sellers' prices to protect you.
- **Banning use of the brand name on the distributor's website** or in search (whole channel) is hardcore (Guidelines para 206 per CMS).

### 1.4 The brand's own D2C site
- The VBER constrains distributors, not the supplier's own sales. If the brand runs its own EU webshop (srisritattva.eu/.de), it may sell passively AND actively in Belgium unless it contractually promises you otherwise. Exclusivity can be "sole" (brand appoints no one else but sells itself) or "exclusive" (brand also refrains from actively selling in BE). The brand promising not to actively target Belgium is lawful. It can never promise to refuse Belgian orders on its own EU site (Geo-blocking Reg applies to the brand too as a trader).
- Dual distribution (brand is also a retailer competing with you) remains block-exempted, but information exchange between brand and distributor is limited (2022 rules: only info necessary for the vertical relationship; your customer-level data and retail prices are sensitive). Relevant if you share sales data.

### 1.5 What "exclusive online distributor for Belgium" means in practice
| Clause | Lawful? | Real value |
|---|---|---|
| Brand appoints no other distributor/reseller in BE | Yes | Medium: removes a local competitor but not EU-wide competitors |
| Other EU distributors barred from active targeting of BE (ads, .be domain, BE campaigns) | Yes (must be imposed by brand on all of them) | Medium-high if the brand actually enforces it |
| Brand's own EU shop does not advertise in BE / redirects BE ad traffic to you | Yes | High, but brand will resist; get it in writing with a remedy |
| All brand distributors barred from Amazon/bol | Yes | High (protects price level) |
| Nobody else may sell to Belgian customers | NO (passive sales, Geo-blocking Art. 6) | Void |
| Brand must refuse Belgian orders on its own site | NO | Void |
| Minimum retail prices for others | NO (RPM) | Void |
| Exclusive for "Belgium online" but brand free to sell via BE pharmacies/shops offline | Yes | Partial |

Conclusion: enforceable exclusivity = "no other appointed seller in my territory + no one actively targets my territory + optionally no marketplaces". Worth: it protects your paid-ad ROI and price level, but organic/passive leakage (esp. a Dutch-language NL shop and a German brand shop) is unavoidable. Belgium (11.8m people, split NL/FR) is small; Dutch-speaking Flemish customers search on .nl shops naturally. Ask for Benelux, not Belgium alone.

### 1.6 Belgian pre-contractual disclosure
- Belgian Code of Economic Law Book X, Title 2 (Art. X.26 ff., "commercial partnership agreements") requires the party granting the right to use a brand/trade name plus commercial assistance to deliver a disclosure document at least one month before signing. Applies to franchise-like and some distribution setups. Non-compliance can allow annulment. FLAG: check with lawyer whether your arrangement qualifies. (General knowledge; not re-verified online in this session.)

Notes:
- Belgian exclusive distribution protection (Art. X.35-X.40): applies to concessions for an indefinite period (a fixed-term contract renewed twice, or tacitly continued, is treated as indefinite per long-standing practice). On termination by supplier (absent serious breach by distributor): reasonable notice (courts awarded 3 to 42 months; ~10-year relationships typically 12-18 months) or equivalent compensation, PLUS an additional "equitable" indemnity: (a) goodwill (substantial increase of clientele that stays with grantor), (b) expenses incurred for the concession that benefit the grantor after termination, (c) staff dismissal costs. Goodwill indemnity is generally refused if sales did not increase significantly. Compensation in lieu of notice is computed from "semi-net profit" (net profit + fixed overheads not reducible). https://cms.law/en/int/expert-guides/cms-expert-guide-to-distribution-law/belgium (snippet), https://www.lexgo.be/fr/actualites-et-articles/3203-be-careful-when-it-comes-to-your-belgian-distributor , https://globallawexperts.com/news/terminate-a-distribution-agreement-in-belgium (promotional, illustrative only)
- Protection covers exclusive AND quasi-exclusive concessions, and those with "important obligations" imposed on the distributor (minimum targets, stock requirements), for sales in all or part of Belgium. A pure online, non-exclusive reseller gets nothing.
- Choice of law/forum: Art. X.39 historically forced Belgian courts/law. Since Cass. 16 Nov 2023 / April 2023 rulings, arbitration is possible and a foreign law chosen under Rome I cannot simply be overridden. The brand (India/German entity) will likely propose German or Indian law, which would remove the Belgian indemnity. NEGOTIATE: Belgian law, Belgian courts. https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/arbitration-blog/belgiums-supreme-court-overturns-decades-old-precedent-and-allows-disputes-about-the-termination-of-exclusive-distribution-agreements-to-be-settled-by-arbitration/ , https://lexgo.be/en/news-and-articles/12761-one-law-to-rule-them-all-the-belgian-exclusive-distribution-act-can-no-longer-overrule-the-law-chosen-by-the-parties
- The Belgian protection only covers the Belgian territory. For other countries: Germany (analogous application of HGB s.89b agent indemnity to integrated distributors, but only if customer data transfer obligation), France (no statutory distributor indemnity; brutal termination of established relationship, Art. L442-1 II C.com, notice up to 18 months), Netherlands (no indemnity for distributors; reasonable notice under case law). So a "network of country webshops" is legally weaker outside Belgium.
- Commercial agent notice per Art. X.16 CEL: 1 month in first 3 years, +1 month per started additional 3-year period (general knowledge; snippet search did not confirm the formula). Goodwill indemnity cap of 1 year's remuneration confirmed: https://www.mondaq.com/data-protection/1322062/vbb-on-belgian-business-law-%7c-volume-2023-no-2

Recommendation: Model A (exclusive distributor, indefinite term, Belgian law) gives the best protection AND exit floor. Model D is the most capital-light and often what a brand prefers (it keeps the customer), but only accept it with a contractual buy-out formula.

### 3.1 Compliance stack per channel
- **Cosmetics (Reg. 1223/2009):** each product needs an EU Responsible Person (RP) established in the EU, a Product Information File and Safety Report (CPSR), and CPNP notification (portal free; service costs typically a few hundred EUR per SKU, FLAG). If the brand's Hamburg entity manufactures, it is the RP. If you import from India yourself, YOU become RP by default. Labels: Belgian law requires NL+FR (and DE in practice) for consumer information. https://ceway.eu/cosmetics-regulatory-services/eu-responsible-person/ , https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/sectors/cosmetics/cosmetic-product-notification-portal_es
- **Ayurvedic-specific risk:** heavy metals (lead, mercury, arsenic in rasa shastra preparations), Annex II prohibited substances, claims ("cures", "treats") that turn a cosmetic/food into an unauthorised medicinal product. Herbal wellness products (capsules, churnas, chyawanprash) are foods/food supplements: in Belgium notification to FPS Public Health (NUT), plant must be on the Belgian plants list (Royal Decree 31/08/2021 on plants), novel food check; FAVV/AFSCA registration for the webshop as a food operator. Several Ayurvedic herbs are restricted. FLAG: SKU-by-SKU regulatory screen needed; this alone can remove 20-40% of the Indian catalogue (estimate).
- **GPSR (Reg. 2023/988, applies since 13 Dec 2024):** for non-food non-cosmetic consumer products the "responsible economic operator" in the EU must be named on product/listing; marketplaces enforce it. Cosmetics and foods are mostly covered by their sector rules but online listing information duties still apply. https://simplyvat.com/?p=8900
- **EPR packaging:**
  - Belgium: Fost Plus (household packaging) obligations apply also to foreign e-sellers shipping to BE consumers; 300 kg de minimis is being phased out / contested under PPWR; simplified e-commerce declaration existed (min. contribution ~EUR 100, 2020 info). https://blog.1cc-consulting.com/en/belgium-simplified-e-commerce-declaration-for-packaging , https://www.ecopv-eu.com/en/packaging-belgium-epr-guideline-2026/
  - Germany: VerpackG, LUCID registration plus dual-system licence from the first package, including shipping boxes; marketplaces must check (general knowledge, not re-verified).
  - France: Citeo (household packaging) and unique identifier (IDU); Triman/Info-tri labelling on products sold in FR; marketplaces liable. (general knowledge)
  - Netherlands: Afvalfonds Verpakkingen, threshold 50,000 kg, so usually exempt for small sellers. (general knowledge)
  - EU PPWR (Reg. 2025/40) applies from 12 Aug 2026, harmonising and requiring authorised representatives for distance sellers in each MS. FLAG: still being implemented nationally.
- **VAT:** EU distance sales threshold EUR 10,000 total cross-border B2C; above it destination VAT via OSS (register in BE). BE VAT: cosmetics 21%, foods 6%. DE 19/7, FR 20/5.5, NL 21/9. Belgian small-enterprise exemption (EUR 25k) only for domestic supplies and excludes OSS benefits. (general knowledge)

## 5. Exit / buy-back

### 5.1 Valuation benchmarks
- Small e-commerce SDE multiples: Empire Flippers 2025 average listing multiple 28.69x monthly (~2.4x annual), sales 23.93x monthly (~2.0x annual); deals < USD 300k around 22x monthly (~1.9x annual). Since Sept 2026 EF quotes annual multiples. Generic ranges 2-3.5x annual SDE; 5-7x aggregator multiples are gone. https://empireflippers.com/value-ecommerce-business/ , https://empireflippers.com/switching-to-annual-multiples/ , https://info.empireflippers.com/hubfs/2026%20Lead%20Magnets/2026%20State%20of%20the%20Industry%20Report.pdf
- Caveat: a single-brand distributor shop has concentration risk (contract can be terminated), so third-party buyers apply a discount; the only natural buyer is the brand, which reduces bargaining power unless the price is pre-agreed.
- Example: SDE EUR 40k x 2-3 = EUR 80-120k + stock at cost. Belgian statutory indemnity can act as a floor (e.g. 12 months semi-net profit in lieu of notice + goodwill indemnity), which is often roughly comparable to a 1-2x multiple; this is the realistic negotiating anchor.

### 5.2 Contract clauses to secure exit value
1. **Indefinite duration (or fixed term with automatic renewal)** and Belgian law + Belgian courts (keeps X.35-X.40 protection). Avoid German/Indian law and foreign arbitration.
2. **Exclusive or quasi-exclusive grant** (explicit), territory BE (ideally Benelux), with brand covenant not to actively target the territory via its own D2C and to redirect territory leads.
3. **Buy-back / call-put option:** brand option to acquire the business after year 3 at a formula (e.g. 2.5-3x trailing 12-month SDE, minimum floor EUR X), and a put option for you on change of control of the brand, launch of brand's own EU D2C targeting BE, or non-renewal.
4. **Termination compensation clause** expressly at least equal to the statutory Belgian indemnity (cannot be waived in advance anyway), plus repurchase of remaining stock at landed cost.
5. **Customer data ownership:** you are GDPR controller of your customers; transfer to the brand only for a price (and lawful basis / notice to customers). Watch for clauses forcing you to share customer data with the brand during the term (also a competition info-exchange issue in dual distribution).
6. **Domain and accounts:** register only your own trade name domain (e.g. ayurveda-house.be), not a brand-name domain, unless the brand gives a written licence; marketplace seller accounts, email lists, social accounts in your company's name, listed as transferable assets.
7. **Trademark licence:** written, non-exclusive or exclusive licence to use Sri Sri Tattva marks and imagery on your site and ads, including keyword bidding on the brand name in the territory.
8. **Change of control:** your right to assign the contract to a buyer of your company (otherwise the business is unsellable to anyone but the brand).
9. **Minimum purchase targets:** keep realistic; failing them is the brand's typical exit route without indemnity ("serious breach").
10. **Most-favoured pricing and supply guarantee** (no worse wholesale terms than other EU distributors; allocation in shortage).
11. **Non-compete after term:** limited to 1 year and to the territory (VBER requires this).
12. **Pre-contractual disclosure (Art. X.26 ff.)** check.

## 6. Risks

- **Bypass risk (main risk):** brand launches its own EU shop from Hamburg (very plausible once production starts), uses your demand data, then terminates or simply outcompetes via passive sales into BE. Mitigation: Belgian-law exclusive concession of indefinite duration, the buy-out formula, keep customer data in your entity, position yourself as the operator of the EU D2C (Model D/E) rather than competitor.
- **Trademark/domain:** registering "srisritattva.be" or similar without consent = trademark infringement and cybersquatting risk (.be disputes via CEPANI; UDRP style), and can be transferred to the brand without compensation. Do not do it without a written licence; if the brand asks you to hold the brand domain, include a transfer-for-value clause.
- **Regulatory:** ayurvedic herbals and heavy metals; unauthorised health claims (Reg. 1924/2006); Belgian plants decree; labelling languages. Enforcement by FAVV/AFSCA and FPS Economy; marketplace delistings.
- **Supply:** Hamburg plant not yet operational (no public evidence); dependency on India imports with customs, RP and shelf-life issues meanwhile.
- **Grey market / existing sellers:** pushmycart and others already sell; Indian-origin grey imports on Amazon. Brand can act against non-EEA-sourced goods via trademark, only if it is willing.
- **Small territory:** Belgium alone gives limited scale; Dutch-language demand naturally flows to NL shops.
- **Indemnity legal uncertainty:** Belgian awards are equitable and unpredictable (3-42 months notice range); goodwill indemnity refused if no significant growth; Rome I choice of law can neutralise it; litigation cost and time (2-4 years).
