## 1. Legal framework

### 1.1 Agent vs "deal finder" / apporteur d'affaires (the key issue)

- Directive 86/653 and its national implementations protect a **commercial agent**: a self-employed intermediary with *continuing* (permanent) authority to *negotiate*, and possibly conclude, sales on behalf of the principal.
- An **apporteur d'affaires** (FR) or occasional intermediary/broker (BE: *makelaar/courtier*) who only introduces leads, with no permanent negotiation mission, is **outside** the statute. That means no mandatory indemnity, no mandatory notice and no statutory post-termination commission. Pay is whatever the contract says.
- French case law: an apporteur without negotiating power cannot obtain requalification as an agent. Conversely, a contract is requalified according to the *reality* of the activity, and the burden of proof sits on the person claiming agent status. In practice, if requalified, the indemnity is usually valued at 2 years of gross commissions. https://kohenavocats.fr/2026/04/28/contrat-apporteur-affaires-commission-rupture-requalification-agent-commercial/ (search snippet)
- CJEU *Trendsetteuse* (C-828/18, 4 June 2020): "negotiate" does not require the power to change the principal's prices or terms. The French Cour de cassation then aligned (Cass. com. 2 Dec 2020). [VERIFY citations] This makes agent status easier to claim for someone who presents the range, handles buyer meetings and pushes listings.
- **Risk in this offer:** "open doors, get paid on deals closed" reads like an apporteur arrangement. If the contract is drafted as "business introducer / referral fee", the principal may avoid the indemnity, notice and tail protections. **Insist on a commercial agency contract** that says in writing that he is "charged on a permanent basis with negotiating sales of the Products in the Territory". The written definition alone does not decide status, though: he also has to actually do that work and keep evidence of it (meeting notes, emails with buyers).

### 1.3 France (Code de commerce L134-1 to L134-17)

- **Notice (L134-11):** 1 month in year 1, 2 months in year 2, 3 months from year 3. The parties cannot shorten it, and if they lengthen it the principal's notice cannot be shorter than the agent's. https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/id/LEGIARTI000006220397/2026-08-05
- **Indemnity (L134-12):** compensation for the loss suffered (a "préjudice" logic, not the Directive's capped "goodwill" logic). The statute sets **no cap**. Courts customarily award **2 years of gross commissions** (average of the last 2 or 3 years), but in May 2026 the Nîmes Court of Appeal awarded 12 months (per a law firm blog [VERIFY]). Lost if not claimed within 1 year, and not due for serious misconduct or for resignation without legitimate cause. Clauses removing it in advance are void (L134-16). https://www.weblex.fr/articles/faire-appel-a-un-agent-commercial-ce-qu-il-faut-savoir ; https://cms.law/fr/fra/a-la-une/agent-commercial-impossibilite-de-renoncer-a-l-rsquo-indemnite-de-cessation-de-contrat-quel-que-soit-le-montage-contractuel ; https://kvk.nl/en/international/what-to-arrange-in-an-agency-agreement
- **Non-compete (L134-14):** in writing, limited to the territory/customers and the type of goods, **max 2 years**, and assessed for proportionality. https://www.baumann-avocats.com/CCOM/article-l134-14-du-code-de-commerce.php
- **Del credere:** French law allows it but courts require it to be limited and paid for [VERIFY]. Refuse it.
- **Registration:** French agents historically had to register on the RSAC. Since 2023 this runs through the Registre national des entreprises / guichet unique [VERIFY]. A Belgium-based agent prospecting in France probably does not need to register in France, but check this.
- **Payment terms law (useful context):** L441-10 caps B2B terms at 60 days from invoice, or 45 days end of month. https://hayot-expertise.fr/en/blog/b2b-payment-terms-france-2026

### 1.4 Choice of law with an Indian or German/Dutch principal

- **CJEU *Ingmar* (C-381/98, 2000):** where the agent works in the EU, the Directive's indemnity and compensation rules (Arts 17 and 18) are mandatory and cannot be avoided by choosing a third-country law (in that case California). The same applies to Indian law. https://curia.europa.eu/en/actu/communiques/cp00/aff/cp0083en.htm
- ***Unamar* (C-184/12, 2013):** a Belgian court may apply Belgian law instead of another Member State's law that was chosen (Bulgarian in that case), if the forum treats its rules as overriding mandatory rules. Belgian Art. X.25 is written that way. https://conflictoflaws.net/2013/ecj-rules-on-freedom-of-member-states-to-consider-statutes-implementing-eu-directives-mandatory-rules/?print=pdf
- **Practical point:** an Indian-law clause with arbitration in India (or a Mumbai/Bangalore forum clause) is a red flag. EU courts would still apply the minimum protections, but enforcing against an Indian entity is slow and expensive. Ask for **Belgian law and Belgian courts** (Brussels or his home district), with the counterparty being the **EU entity** (B.V. or GmbH) and a parent company guarantee if possible. Dutch law (BW 7:442, 1-year cap) or German law (HGB 89b, 1-year cap) are acceptable fallbacks. Both implement the Directive.
- **French territory under Belgian law:** if Belgian law governs the whole contract, French customers fall under Belgian rules (1-year cap plus additional damages). That is arguably *less* generous than French case-law practice (about 2 years). Alternative: a separate French agency contract under French law for France. Weigh this against simplicity. [lawyer question]

### 1.5 Clauses he must insist on (checklist)

1. **Status:** "commercial agent" under CEL Book X / Directive 86/653, with permanent negotiation authority in the Territory. Not "business introducer" or "consultant".
2. **Territory and exclusivity:** exclusive for Belgium (and Luxembourg if possible) and France, or at minimum for named channels (organic, pharmacy, drugstore, grocery). This triggers commission on **all** sales to customers in the territory, including direct, webshop B2B and distributor re-sales into the territory (Art. 7(2)). Without exclusivity, fall back to a **named-account schedule** of every account he has introduced, updated monthly and countersigned.
3. **Commission rate and base:** a % of **net invoiced sales** (ex-VAT, after invoice discounts). Specify whether back-margin, listing fees and promo funding paid by the principal are or are not deducted. They should **not** reduce his base unless capped. Use a separate (lower) rate for sales through a distributor, calculated on the principal's invoice to the distributor.
4. **Repeat orders:** commission on **all repeat orders for life of the contract**, not just the first order.
5. **Tail ("payable over X years"):** this is contractual on top of the statutory indemnity, and the indemnity cannot be waived. Ask for the tail to run **at least 2 to 3 years after termination on introduced accounts, at full rate, or tapering (100/75/50%)**. Make sure the tail does **not** count against the indemnity.
6. **Accrual and payment:** commission is earned when the customer is invoiced (or at the latest when the customer pays). The Directive says it is due at the latest when the third party has executed or should have executed. Monthly or quarterly statements, payment within 30 days of quarter-end, and late interest at the statutory rate.
7. **Audit/information rights:** a monthly sales report by customer and SKU, plus the statutory right to a book extract and an independent audit at the principal's cost if the discrepancy is above 5%.
8. **Bypass protection:** commission is due on any sale to a customer in the territory, or to a customer he introduced, however it is concluded (directly, via another distributor, via a webshop B2B account or via an affiliate).
9. **No minimum targets as termination triggers** in years 1 and 2. Any target from year 3 should be conditional on the principal delivering stock, EU-compliant labels, a marketing/promo budget and competitive pricing.
10. **Principal obligations:** stock availability with lead times, EU compliance (CPNP notifications for cosmetics; food supplement notifications in BE via FOODSUP and in FR via Teleicare), FR/NL labels, samples, price lists, a listing-fee and promo budget, and prompt acceptance or refusal of orders. If the principal refuses an order without good reason, the commission is still due [VERIFY under BE law].
11. **Advance/retainer or expense contribution:** ask for a monthly retainer (EUR 500 to 1,500) or reimbursement of trade fair costs for the first 12 months. Without this he is financing the principal's market entry.
12. **No del credere**, and no liability for customer non-payment beyond losing commission on that unpaid invoice.
13. **Non-compete:** limited to directly competing Ayurvedic products. He keeps the right to carry complementary non-competing brands. This matters, because a multi-brand portfolio is how agents make a living.
14. **Duration and termination:** indefinite duration (or a fixed term converting to indefinite) with statutory notice. Only serious-cause termination without notice, and the clientele indemnity explicitly preserved.
15. **Governing law Belgian, Belgian courts, EU counterparty**, and a parent company guarantee.
16. **Change of control/brand sale:** if the brand or its EU business is sold, the contract transfers, or termination triggers the indemnity plus the tail.

## 2. Commission benchmarks and retailer economics

### 2.1 Commission rates (benchmarks; weak public data)

- **No authoritative public benchmark** was found for FMCG, health or beauty agents in BE/FR. In Germany, if no rate is agreed, the customary industry rate applies (HGB 87b). https://www.ihk.de/ostwestfalen/recht-und-steuern/recht-von-a-z/grundzuege-des-handelsvertreterrechts-6770884
- A French legal guide gives 5 to 20% depending on sector, basket and volume, with degressive examples (10% up to EUR 500k, 7% above). It is unsourced. https://www.swim.legal/blog/agent-commercial-salaire-commissions-remuneration-fiscalite
- Industry practice (from knowledge, [VERIFY]):
  - Grocery and mass retail (GMS) agents: **3 to 7%**, because volumes are high and chains negotiate centrally.
  - Organic/specialist and pharmacy/parapharmacy agents for small brands: **8 to 15%**, sometimes 15 to 20% for independent-store prospecting.
  - Commission on sales to a distributor: **3 to 8%** of the principal's invoice to the distributor.
  - Affiliate health and beauty rates of 9 to 15% (Tradedoubler) are not comparable. https://knowledge.tradedoubler.com/grow-knowledge/what-commission-rates-do-you-recommend
- **Recommendation:** 10 to 12% on net sales to direct retail/pharmacy accounts, 5 to 7% on distributor invoices, and a +2 point bonus above an agreed annual threshold. If no retainer is paid, ask for 15% in year 1.

## 5. Viability: income scenarios (gross commission, before costs, social contributions and tax)

Assumptions: 10% on direct retail invoices, 6% on distributor invoices. No retainer. Belgian self-employed social contributions are about 20.5% of net professional income [VERIFY], then income tax. Own costs (car, travel to France, samples logistics, one or two fairs) are EUR 5,000 to 15,000 per year unless the principal pays them.

| Scenario | Year 1 territory sales (principal invoice) | Y1 commission | Y2 sales | Y2 commission | Y3 sales | Y3 commission |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Low** (compliance problems, stock gaps, only shops and ethnic) | EUR 20k | **EUR 1.5-2k** | EUR 50k | **EUR 4-5k** | EUR 80k | **EUR 6-8k** |
| **Base** (wholesaler + 1 or 2 organic chains + parapharmacy test) | EUR 60k | **EUR 4-6k** | EUR 200k | **EUR 14-18k** | EUR 400k | **EUR 28-35k** |
| **High** (BE wholesaler + Medi-Market/Di + FR wholesaler + one grocery listing, with marketing budget) | EUR 150k | **EUR 10-13k** | EUR 600k | **EUR 40-50k** | EUR 1.2m | **EUR 80-100k** |

- **Net of costs, Year 1 is likely negative or near zero in the low and base cases.** This is a part-time or side-portfolio activity unless he gets a retainer or carries 3 to 5 complementary brands (non-competing natural/wellness brands) to spread fixed costs. That is how most independent agents make it work.
- The value is back-loaded: repeat orders plus the exclusive-territory commission plus the tail plus the indemnity (1 year average under BE law) make years 3 to 5 the payoff. That only happens if the contract is right **and the principal stays the course**.

### Key failure modes

1. **Regulatory/product fit:** core Ayurvedic SKUs (ashwagandha, churnas, tablets) are not compliant or need reformulation. There are no FR/NL labels or CPNP/supplement notifications.
2. **Stock and supply:** Indian supply chains have long lead times. The "Hamburg production" is unverified. Stock-outs after listing lead to delisting and penalties.
3. **No marketing/listing budget:** chains expect listing fees, promotions, sampling and in-store activation. Unknown brands without consumer pull get delisted in 3 to 6 months.
4. **Price competitiveness:** against Himalaya (via Phoenix Import), Weleda, Dr. Hauschka, Lavera, Logona, Yogi Tea, Pukka (Unilever) and retailer private labels. Indian MRP-based pricing plus import cost plus distributor plus retailer margins may land above EUR 10 for a toothpaste. Benchmark early.
5. **Bypass:** the principal deals directly with accounts after the introduction, or appoints a distributor who takes over the accounts. Mitigate with an exclusive territory (Art. 7(2)), the named-account schedule, the tail and audit rights.
6. **Mis-classification:** the contract is drafted as "introducer", so there is no indemnity, no notice and no tail.
7. **Counterparty risk:** an Indian entity with Indian law and an Indian forum makes enforcement impractical. The principal changes its EU strategy or entity, or the founder-led/spiritual-organisation governance changes direction.
8. **Personal network gap:** zero BE/FR buyer contacts. The only French contact fell out with the brand, so that relationship is a liability. Check *why* they fell out (unpaid commission? stock?). It is the best due-diligence data point available.
9. **Brand perception:** association with a spiritual movement (Art of Living) may help community sales but put off some mainstream buyers.
