# 07 - Art of Living (AOL) ecosystem: legal structure and money flows

Research date: 2026-11-23. Method: web search snippets only. Direct page fetches were blocked by the session network (caravanmagazine.in and projects.propublica.org failed to resolve; MCA21, Zefix, Handelsregister, KBO, CRA and FCRA portals were not reachable). Every figure below therefore comes from search-result snippets of the cited URLs, and anything taken from a data aggregator (Tofler, CauseIQ, Impala, Trendlyne, MarketScreener, give.do) should be treated as secondary until checked against the primary registry.

Key takeaway: AOL is not one legal entity. It is a brand used by a loose federation of (a) Indian public charitable trusts, (b) national non-profits abroad (US 501(c)(3)s, a UK charitable company, a Dutch stichting, a German e.V., a Canadian charity, a Swiss foundation), and (c) a separate set of for-profit companies in India (the "Sumeru" group and Sriveda Sattva / Sri Sri Tattva) and in Switzerland (Shankara Universal GmbH). No public document found shows a shareholding chain linking the charities to the companies. The visible links are shared brand, shared address, shared people (notably the founder's nephew N. Arvind Varchaswi and brother-in-law Narayanan Narasimhan in the companies) and, per a 2016 investigation, foreign-contribution transfers from overseas affiliates to Indian trusts.

## 1. Governance primer: trustees vs directors vs shareholders

- **Trust (India, Indian Trusts Act 1882 / public charitable trust):** trustees hold property for a charitable purpose set in the trust deed. There are no owners and no shareholders. Trustees may not take profit; surplus must be applied to the objects. Tax exemption comes from registration under Income Tax Act s.12A/12AB and donor deduction under s.80G. Receipt of foreign money requires FCRA registration with the Ministry of Home Affairs.
- **Section 8 company (India):** a non-profit company limited by shares or guarantee; has members and directors but cannot pay dividends. None found in the AOL ecosystem.
- **Private limited company (India, "Pvt Ltd", CIN ending PTC):** owned by shareholders, run by directors appointed by shareholders. Profits can be paid out as dividends to shareholders. Directors and shareholders can be the same people.
- **US 501(c)(3):** non-profit corporation with a board of directors; no owners; must file Form 990 (public).
- **UK charitable company:** company limited by guarantee plus charity registration; its directors are also its charity trustees.
- **Swiss Stiftung / foundation:** ownerless endowment governed by a Stiftungsrat (foundation board); supervised by a cantonal or federal foundation authority.
- **German e.V. / gGmbH:** registered association (members, board) / non-profit GmbH (shareholders, but profits locked to charitable purpose).
- **Dutch stichting:** foundation with a board (bestuur), no members or owners; ANBI status gives tax-deductibility.

So: in AOL's trusts, people like Bhanumathi Narasimhan or named trustees "control" assets but do not own them. In the companies (Sriveda Sattva, Sumeru entities), the directors and especially the shareholders do own the economic value, and profits could legally flow as dividends to whoever holds the shares. Who holds those shares is the central unanswered question (see FLAGS).
