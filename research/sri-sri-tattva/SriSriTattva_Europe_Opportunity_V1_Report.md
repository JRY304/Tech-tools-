# Sri Sri Tattva Europe: Opportunity Assessment V1

Scope: freelance sales role (BE/FR), the reported acquisition and Hamburg production, product range and EU fit, the online shop, a Belgian webshop with exclusivity and exit.
Method: six parallel desk-research streams. Source files with URLs are in `sources/`. The research sandbox blocked direct page loads for most registries and all shop sites, so registry and price data come from search-result snippets, not primary documents. Treat every number as indicative until verified.

---

## 1. Bottom line

**The commission-only role, as described, is not a viable primary income.** Base case is roughly EUR 5k gross commission in year 1, EUR 16k in year 2 and EUR 30k in year 3, against EUR 5k to 15k a year of your own costs. It only becomes worth doing if four conditions hold: you are contracted as a **commercial agent** (not a business introducer), you get a **retainer or expense cover**, the contract is under **Belgian law with the EU entity as counterparty**, and the range you sell is **verified EU-compliant**.

**The webshop-with-exclusivity idea is weaker than it looks**, because Sri Sri Tattva already runs an EU shop (srisritattva.eu, operated by Sri Sri Tattva Europe B.V., The Hague) that ships to Belgium. EU law lets them give you Belgian exclusivity against other distributors, but never against Belgian customers who order from their shop on their own. A Belgium-only webshop looks like EUR 20k to 100k revenue in year 1 and is not a standalone business.

**The real swing factor is the acquisition.** If the acquired company is what the evidence points to (AMLA Natur Vertriebs GmbH near Hamburg: 1,000+ products, BDIH-certified natural cosmetics, EU organic foods, already in German online pharmacies), you would be selling an EU-made, EU-compliant range with a track record. That is a far easier sell to Belgian and French buyers than the Indian catalogue, much of which is blocked or risky under EU rules. Nothing public confirms the acquisition. Verify it before anything else.

**Recommendation:** do not commit time on a pure-commission basis yet. Get four answers from the director (section 9), then negotiate an exclusive Benelux distributorship or agency under Belgian law, starting with the cosmetics/personal-care line and the acquired brand's range.

## 6. The freelance sales role (Pillar 2)

**The legal trap.** "Open doors, get paid on deals closed" is typically drafted as an *apporteur d'affaires* (business introducer). That status has no mandatory notice, no goodwill indemnity and no commission on repeat orders. You must be contracted as a **commercial agent** with permanent authority to negotiate. Under the CJEU's Trendsetteuse ruling (2020) you do not need power over prices to qualify.

| | Belgium (Code of Economic Law, Book X) | France (Code de commerce L134) |
|---|---|---|
| Notice | 1 month per started year, max 6 | 1, 2 or 3 months |
| Goodwill indemnity | up to 1 year's average remuneration (X.18), plus proven damages (X.19) | no legal cap; courts usually award about 2 years' commission (one 2026 ruling gave 12 months) |
| Non-compete after end | max 6 months | max 2 years |
| Foreign law clause | Belgian law and courts apply mandatorily to an agent based in Belgium (X.25); Ingmar ruling protects EU agents even under Indian law | same mandatory protection |

**Clauses to insist on:** exclusive territory, so you earn commission on **all** sales into it, including the brand's own webshop orders from Belgium (Directive Art. 7(2)); commission on net invoiced sales including repeat orders; a 2 to 3 year post-term tail **on top of** the indemnity; audit rights; anti-bypass; no del credere (you guaranteeing customers pay); no sales minimums in year 1; a retainer or expense cover; Belgian law and courts; the EU entity as counterparty, ideally with a parent guarantee.

**Commission benchmarks** (industry practice, no authoritative public source): about 3 to 7% for supermarket channels, 8 to 15% for organic shops and pharmacies, 3 to 8% on sales through a distributor. Ask for 10 to 12% direct, 5 to 7% via distributors, or 15% in year 1 if there's no retainer. Retailer margins of 35 to 50%, distributor margins of 25 to 35%, listing fees and 60-day payment terms all squeeze the brand's ability to pay you.

**Target accounts, realistic order:**
1. **Start:** Färm, Biofresh (wholesaler supplying hundreds of Belgian organic shops), Ankorstore, Indian grocers, Art of Living centres and yoga studios.
2. **Next:** Medi-Market, Di, Bio-Planet, Holland & Barrett Belgium, French organic wholesalers, Nature & Découvertes.
3. **Long shots:** Kruidvat, Colruyt, Carrefour, Albert Heijn, Biocoop central buying, French pharmacy groupements. Aldi and Lidl are not a fit.

**Benchmark:** no Indian Ayurvedic brand has mainstream BE/FR supermarket listings. Himalaya entered via a distributor (Phoenix Import); Patanjali via ADF Foods UK, mostly ethnic channels; Kama Ayurveda is moving slowly with Puig's backing.

**From zero network:** trade fairs (Biofach/Vivaness Nuremberg 16 to 19 Feb 2027; Natexpo Paris 26 to 28 Sep 2027), going after distributors before chains, and using the Art of Living community as the first proof of sell-through. Expect 6 to 12 months to a first meaningful deal, 12 to 18+ for chains.

**Income scenarios** (gross commission, before your costs and about 20.5% social contributions):

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | ~EUR 2k | ~EUR 5k | ~EUR 7k |
| Base | ~EUR 5k | ~EUR 16k | ~EUR 30k |
| High | ~EUR 12k | ~EUR 45k | ~EUR 90k |

**The French contact.** He is your best due-diligence source, more than an asset. Before reviving him, find out exactly why they fell out: unpaid commission, stock failures and the brand bypassing him are the three failure modes that matter. If it was any of those, it predicts your experience. If he is still useful, structure him as your sub-agent for France under your contract, so the brand cannot cut you out of the French channel.

## 7. Webshop, exclusivity and exit (Pillar 3b)

**What exclusivity you can actually get** (Vertical Block Exemption Regulation 2022/720; the brand is far below the 30% market-share threshold):
- **Can:** be the exclusive distributor for Belgium, better for Benelux, since Flemish buyers shop on Dutch sites. The brand can forbid other EU distributors from **actively** targeting Belgium (Belgian ads, .be domains, Belgian campaigns). The brand can lawfully ban all distributors from Amazon and bol (Coty; 2022 Guidelines).
- **Cannot:** ban passive sales (unsolicited orders from Belgians to other EU sellers), ban online sales, fix your retail price. The Geo-blocking Regulation voids clauses forcing traders to refuse unsolicited cross-border orders.
- **The brand's own shop:** can always serve Belgian buyers who find it. The most you can get is a contractual promise that it won't actively target Belgium, plus commission or a margin share on its Belgian orders.

**Which structure:**

| | Stock risk | Termination protection | Asset to sell | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Commercial agent | none | notice + up to 1 year's commission | no | low capital, low upside |
| **Exclusive distributor, indefinite term, Belgian law** | yes | Book X Art. X.35 to X.40 (former 1961 law): reasonable notice (courts have given 3 to 42 months) or compensation, plus indemnity for goodwill, expenses and staff | yes | **best for building something sellable** |
| Operator of their EU D2C shop | none | none unless contracted | only via buy-out clause | capital-light, fragile |

A foreign-law or arbitration clause can neutralise the Belgian distributor protection. Refuse German or Indian law.

**Marketplaces:** Amazon (8% up to EUR 10, 15% beauty), bol (15 to 22% reported, unverified), Douglas marketplace (about 10%, dated), Redcare/Farmaline marketplace. Newpharma, Notino, Ecco Verde and Holland & Barrett buy as retailers, so you'd approach them as a supplier, which requires distributor status. Compliance stack: cosmetics Responsible Person and CPNP, GPSR operator details, packaging EPR (Fost Plus BE, LUCID DE, Citeo/Triman FR; EU packaging regulation PPWR from Aug 2026), VAT OSS above EUR 10k cross-border.

**Unit economics:** on a EUR 40 order including VAT at about 50% gross margin, roughly EUR 9.75 is left before marketing; paid acquisition costs EUR 20 to 40 per customer. It only works on repeat purchase and cheap community channels. Not viable as a full-time Belgium-only business.

**Exit:** small e-commerce sells at about 2 to 3x annual seller's discretionary earnings. The brand is the only natural buyer, so a sale only has value if the price is fixed in the contract upfront:
- a buy-back option with a price formula, plus a put option if control of the brand changes or it launches its own shop targeting your territory
- termination compensation at least equal to the Belgian statutory indemnity, and stock bought back at cost
- you own the customer data, domains and accounts
- a written trademark licence (do not register brand-name domains without one; that is infringement)
- the right to assign the contract to a buyer of your company

**The "network of webshops across Europe" idea:** the brand already has a pan-EU shop and is setting up a German operating company. Pitching to replace that is pitching against their own plan. A more credible pitch: localised NL/FR storefronts and marketplace operation for Benelux and France, with a contractual buy-out. That is a service they lack, not a competitor to what they're building.

## 8. Flags

**Verify before committing (high impact):**
1. **The acquisition.** No public trace. Get the legal name of the acquired company. If it is AMLA Natur, pull the Handelsregister shareholder list (HRB 4209; court listed as Tostedt, possibly now Lüneburg) for 2025/26 changes.
2. **Hamburg production.** Only signal is the April 2026 Frankfurt GmbH's stated purpose. Ask for site, start date and which SKUs will be made there.
3. **Which entity signs your contract,** and its balance sheet. The B.V. has EUR 10k capital.
4. **The brand's plans for srisritattva.eu in Belgium.** This decides whether exclusivity is worth anything.
5. **Why the French contact fell out with them.**

**Data quality (medium):**
6. All registry and financial data comes from aggregator snippets; revenue estimates conflict (INR 300 to 400 crore vs other figures). No audited FY25/26 numbers.
7. Board composition conflicts across sources (Katpitia, Bagga).
8. Product research conflicted on EU presence: one stream said no official EU distribution; two others found srisritattva.eu and the B.V. as official EU importer. The latter is better supported.
9. SKU counts could be off by about 30%; Amazon.de/.nl, Dookan and Jamoona listings unverified.
10. India's dairy export authorisation to the EU (decides ghee and Chyawanprash) unconfirmed.
11. Commission benchmarks, retailer margins, marketplace fees and income scenarios are industry practice and estimates, not primary data.
12. Belgian notice and indemnity figures are court-practice ranges, not statutory formulas.
13. Not found or unconfirmed: "Ecoplanet", "Vivaco", Health & Wellness Expo Brussels; conflicting 2027 dates for Natural & Organic Products Europe.

**Concerns about the business model and organisation:**
14. **Commission-only with a multi-year payout puts all the risk on you** and gives the brand a free option on your network. Bypass risk is high with a centralised principal that already sells direct.
15. **Organisational churn** (multiple EU entities, a 2022 insolvency, shrinking headcount) raises the chance that the person who signs your deal is gone before your tail payments end.
16. **Reputational exposure:** heavy-metal and allergen incidents in the wider Ayurveda category, and the Art of Living association, will come up with pharmacy buyers.
17. **No public data on how reliably they pay agents or distributors.** Ask the French contact and any existing DACH distributor.

Get a Belgian commercial agency/distribution lawyer to review any contract before signing. This report is not legal advice.

## 9. What to do next

1. **Ask the director four questions in writing:** the legal name of the acquired company; the Hamburg production timeline and SKU list; which entity would contract with you; whether srisritattva.eu will add NL/FR and target Belgium.
2. **Call the French contact** about why it broke down, before discussing a revival.
3. **Run the 5-minute checkout test yourself** on .com and .eu with a Belgian address.
4. **If the answers hold up,** counter-propose an exclusive Benelux agency or distributorship under Belgian law: commission on all Belgian sales including web orders, a retainer for the first 12 months, a 2 to 3 year tail, a buy-out formula, and an initial range limited to cosmetics, personal care, incense and the acquired brand's line.
5. **If they refuse a retainer and Belgian law,** treat it as a side activity at most, alongside other brands, not a business to build.
