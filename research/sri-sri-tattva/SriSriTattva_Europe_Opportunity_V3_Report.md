## 3. Contract mechanics, answered

**Commercial agent.** A protected legal status (Belgian Code of Economic Law, Book X) for an independent with a standing mandate to find and negotiate deals for a principal, paid by commission. It brings mandatory notice (1 month per started year, max 6), a goodwill indemnity of up to one year's average commission, and a right to see the figures. The alternative they will likely propose, a business introducer (*apporteur d'affaires*), gets none of that. A BV can be a commercial agent, so BV to BV is fine.

**Which of their entities signs.** Not automatically "their BV". There are at least three candidates: Sri Sri Tattva Europe B.V. (NL), Sri Sri Tattva (Germany) GmbH, and AMLA Natur Vertriebs GmbH. Contract with the entity that invoices Belgian customers, or with all of them, otherwise you cannot verify your commission. Ask for a parent-company guarantee: the B.V. has EUR 10k capital.

**Commission on their web orders.** You do not get this automatically. EU and Belgian law entitle an agent with an *exclusive* territory to commission on sales to customers in that territory even without the agent's involvement, but this is generally treated as excludable by contract, and they will try to exclude their own webshop. Write it in explicitly, typically at a lower rate (3 to 5%) than deals you close (10 to 12%).

**How you check it.** The right to a commission statement at least quarterly, and to extracts from their books to verify it, is mandatory and cannot be contracted away. In practice: a monthly order export from their shop (Shopify) filtered on shipping country Belgium, plus their B2B invoice list for Belgian customers. Five minutes of work for them; a refusal is a signal.

**Retainer.** For a part-time market opener, EUR 1,000 to 2,500 a month for 6 to 12 months is a reasonable ask. Alternatives: an advance deducted from later commission (a "draw"), or them paying samples, fair stands, travel and NL/FR materials. Expect refusal of cash given how they operate; then push for 15% in year 1 and full cost coverage.

**Tail payments.** Separate from the statutory indemnity: commission on repeat orders from customers you brought in, for 2 to 3 years after the contract ends. Make it explicitly *on top of* the indemnity.

**Sellable exit.** Only exists if you own something: a webshop, stock, customer contracts in your name. As an agent your exit is the indemnity plus the tail. If you want a buy-out later, it has to be a distributor or webshop-operator structure with the price formula (e.g. 2 to 3x the last 12 months' profit) written in from day one.

**Non-compete.** Expect a clause forbidding competing brands during the contract (lawful) and possibly after (Belgian law caps a post-contract non-compete for agents at 6 months and restricts it to the same territory and goods). This directly limits the multi-brand shop idea.

## 4. What you would actually do: market-opening playbook

The job has three parts: **open** accounts, **service** them (reorders, shelf, staff training, promotions, complaints), and **report** back. Opening is what they say they want; servicing is the part that creates recurring value and the part they will want to take over. Your commission must follow the account, not the first order.

### Phase 0: before signing (month 0 to 1)

- Contract terms per section 3.
- Get the **sellable range for Belgium in writing**: which SKUs are EU-compliant, have NL/FR labels, and (for supplements) have been notified to FPS Public Health via FOODSUP. Without this you sell nothing. Ashwagandha is reportedly not allowed in Belgian supplements.
- Get the **trade terms**: wholesale price list, retailer margin (expect 40 to 50% needed), minimum order (EUR 150 to 250 for small shops), delivery time to Belgium, who handles orders and invoices, returns policy.
- Get a **sample and materials budget**: sample kits, NL/FR product sheets, a price list in EUR, a B2B order form or portal.

## 5. The online side

**Who runs it.** Sri Sri Tattva Europe B.V. runs srisritattva.eu as a single Shopify store for all of Europe, shipping from (likely) Waalwijk. Nobody is assigned to Belgium. The B.V. also sells on bol.com and Amazon.com.be. AMLA runs its own DE/EN shops and does not localise for Belgium either.

**Three ways you could be involved online, ranked:**

1. **Commission on Belgian web orders** (section 3). No work, no risk, pure upside. Ask for it first.
2. **Operate the Benelux/France online channel for them as a service:** French storefront, NL/FR content, bol.com and Amazon BE/FR listings, customer service. Paid as a fee plus revenue share, with a buy-out formula. This fills their actual gap (no French, no localisation, sold-out stock) and does not compete with them. Capital-light. Risk: no legal protection unless contracted.
3. **Your own multi-brand Ayurveda shop for Belgium.** See below. Weakest of the three.

**The multi-brand shop, tested:**

- **Marketplace ("bol.com of Ayurveda")**: not feasible. A marketplace needs many sellers and many buyers at the same time; brands already reach Belgian buyers through bol.com, Amazon and 25 specialist shops.
- **Competition:** Belgian specialists (Ananda Ayurveda, Mala India, Herboristerie Sanjivani, Art of Vedas), Dutch specialists shipping to Belgium (Pranayur largest at about 332 Trustpilot reviews, Holisan, De Ayurveda Specialist, Surya, Maharishi Ayurveda NL), the Austrian niceshops group (Ayurveda101, VitalAbo, Ecco Verde, which already carry Classic Ayurveda), generalists (Holland & Barrett, Newpharma, Kazidomi, bol.com).
- **The gap:** no sizeable Belgian-owned, bilingual NL/FR, advice-led Ayurveda shop. Small review counts across the board suggest a niche market.
- **Feasible model:** curated hybrid. Stock 3 to 6 fast movers, dropship the long tail, sell B2B to the same practitioners and studios. Unit economics from V1 apply: about EUR 9.75 left per EUR 40 order before marketing, paid acquisition EUR 20 to 40, so it only works on repeat customers and community channels.
- **Conflict:** carrying Maharishi, Himalaya or others while holding a Sri Sri Tattva/AMLA mandate will likely breach the non-compete. Note that AMLA itself already sells competing brands, so negotiate the right to sell **AMLA's existing third-party range** too: that gives you multi-brand breadth with one supplier and no conflict.

## 12. Webshop vs agency, revisited

**Why the agency was recommended first:**
- The premise "people find your webshop" is the weak point. Building the shop is easy; **traffic is the cost** (EUR 20 to 40 per customer via paid ads), and you compete with about 25 shops plus the brand's own site at the same price on a 30 to 50% margin.
- Most category volume flows through shops, pharmacies and wholesalers. Agency commission covers all of it with zero capital; webshop income covers only your own site.
- They are not mutually exclusive: agency first, webshop later as your consumer channel once trade terms and the sellable SKU list are known.

**What Shopify does and does not do:** Shopify provides the website, checkout, payments and apps; you do not need a separate website. It does **not** remove stock. Stock sits with you, a fulfilment warehouse (3PL), or the supplier shipping on your behalf (dropshipping). Dropshipping is closest to "no stock" but needs the brand's agreement and gives the thinnest margin.

**The Norway model (importer, then distribution):** fits Norway because it is outside the EU and someone must clear customs. From Drestedt to Belgium there is no import. The Belgian equivalent is a **master distributor**: holds local stock, handles Belgian labelling and FOODSUP notification, and supplies several channel wholesalers (e.g. Mannavita for pharmacy, Biofresh for organic). Your instinct (one importer, several downstream distributors) is the standard structure. The master-distributor role builds a sellable asset but needs capital for stock.

**5-year contract with declining commission:**
- After 5 years you are not simply "done". Under Belgian agency law, when the contract ends other than through your fault or resignation, you are owed a goodwill indemnity of up to one year's average commission if your customers still bring business. A fixed-term contract that keeps running after expiry becomes indefinite (notice period applies).
- Declining commission ("sunset") is legal and common, but it lowers the average the indemnity is calculated on.
- Counter: decline only on accounts you stop servicing; a floor rate (e.g. never below 5%); indemnity calculated on the best years.
- Clarify: 5 years from **each deal**, or 5 years for the **whole contract**? Large difference.

**Three angles to secure in any negotiation:** commission on Belgian web orders; the right to sell AMLA's whole range (including its third-party brands); a first right to become Belgian master distributor later (the bridge to the webshop and to a sellable exit).

## 13. Marketplaces: bol.com, Ayurveda101, and the chicken-and-egg problem

**bol.com:**
- Founded 30 March 1999 by Bertelsmann as a pan-European online shop; retreated to the Netherlands; Belgium added later. Ahold (now Ahold Delhaize) bought it in 2012 for about EUR 350M. Partial IPO planned in 2021, postponed 2022. CEO Maite Zubiaurre from 2025.
- **Benelux only** (NL and BE). About EUR 5.9B net consumer online sales in 2024 (secondary source, unverified). Over 30,000 partner sellers (possibly dated). Much smaller than Amazon overall, but number one in the Netherlands.
- **Why it became so well known:** it was *the* Dutch-language online shop years before Amazon arrived (Amazon.nl 2020, Amazon.com.be 2022), heavy TV marketing, and Ahold's backing. It had the customers before Amazon came.
- **Two ways onto bol:** (1) bol buys your stock as a supplier (bol decides; mostly established brands); (2) **partner seller**: free registration, commission per sale varying by category, Belgian KBO registration and VAT number required, no minimum to start. You ship yourself, or use **Logistiek via bol (LVB)**: bol stores, ships and handles returns; requires at least 1,500 shipments a year for NL/BE partners.
- **Compliance before listing:** cosmetics notified in the EU cosmetics portal (CPNP) with a Responsible Person; supplements notified to the Belgian health ministry (FOODSUP). bol enforces this.
- Correction to the earlier picture: bol does do fulfilment (LVB). Its core value is that millions of shoppers arrive for free.

**Ayurveda101:** one of the in-house shops of **niceshops GmbH** (Saaz, Styria, Austria; also Graz and Vienna; about EUR 169M group revenue in 2025). Multi-brand Ayurveda shop in 9 languages, own warehouse, professional operations. The "Ayurveda shop for all of Europe" already exists, run by a serious operator. This is the benchmark to beat.

**The chicken-and-egg problem:** applies to a **marketplace** (like bol): outside sellers (brands) will not join a site with no shoppers, and shoppers will not come to a site with no range. It does *not* mean retailers will not buy from you. If you buy stock and sell it yourself (curated shop), the supply side is solved: you are the range. What remains is getting the first customers.

**How a small player starts:**
1. Start from a group that already trusts you: the Art of Living community in BE/NL/FR, thousands of people who know these products. Your unfair advantage.
2. Practitioners: Camille's Ayurvedic doctors recommend; patients order online with a practitioner code; the practitioner earns a share.
3. Be the seller yourself before inviting others (bol started with books before opening to partners).
4. Subscriptions on repeat products (toothpaste, teas, oils, Triphala): pay to acquire once, earn for years.

**What makes one of ten equally good websites win:**
1. **Cheap customers of its own**: community, email list, practitioner referrals, search content in Dutch and French. Whoever depends on paid ads loses.
2. **Better supply**: lower buying price, brands others cannot get, always in stock.
3. **Trust and expertise**: real Ayurvedic advice, consultations, a dosha quiz, a known face (Camille's doctor network).
4. **Repeat purchase**: subscriptions and reminders so each customer is worth more than they cost.
5. **Fast, cheap local delivery and the expected payment methods** (Bancontact in Belgium).
6. **Compliance**: notified and correctly labelled products; competitors with unnotified supplements get delisted or reported.
7. **Discipline**: weekly numbers (acquisition cost, reorder rate, margin).
Your team could have 1 and 3; few competitors do. 2 and 4 depend on supply contracts you do not have yet.

## 14. Registration, bol.com and exclusivity

**Question:** if I get products registered in Belgium, can bol.com sell and ship them, and do I get exclusivity?

**Short answer:** yes to bol selling and shipping (as partner seller with LVB). **No to exclusivity**: registration is a legal permission, not an exclusive right.

- **Registration is per product, not per seller.** Cosmetics: one CPNP notification by the Responsible Person, valid EU-wide. Since production will be in the EU (Drestedt), the manufacturer (AMLA or Sri Sri Tattva) will normally be the Responsible Person, not you. Supplements: notified once to FOODSUP by the operator placing the product on the Belgian market; other resellers of the identical product do not have to re-notify. So holding the registration gives at most a small practical head start, not a lock.
- Camille's registrations had more value in the old situation (imports from India), when someone in Europe had to take responsibility. With EU production that role moves to the manufacturer.
- **bol gives no exclusivity:** one product page per EAN barcode; every seller with the same product competes for the "buy box" on that page.

**Where real exclusivity comes from:**
1. **From the brand, by contract:** exclusive distributor for Belgium. The brand may lawfully forbid its other distributors from selling on marketplaces (Coty; 2022 Vertical Guidelines), which would make you the only authorised bol seller in practice. Passive sales cannot be banned, and genuine EU-sourced stock can be resold by anyone (exhaustion of rights). Grey imports from India not placed on the EU market with the brand's consent can be blocked under trademark law, but only the brand can act.
2. **Your own brand (private label):** products under your own name and EAN, made by a contract manufacturer. Nobody else can sell them. **New idea worth exploring:** ask whether AMLA's Drestedt factory would produce for you under your own label (oils, teas, churnas). That gives full exclusivity on bol, Amazon and your own shop, and an asset that is yours to sell.

## 15. Art of Living: trusts, control and money (your questions)

**Trustees vs owners:**
- Trustees never own trust assets. Under Indian tax law, trustees and their relatives may receive **reasonable** pay and benefits; unreasonable benefits cost the trust its tax exemption, which is the main check.
- Trusts can buy cars and houses; these remain trust property, used by trustees.
- **On dissolution** of a public charitable trust, assets cannot go to trustees or founders; they go to another charity with similar objects. Indian tax law imposes an exit tax if a charitable trust converts to a non-charity.
- **Where value can legally move** without ownership: control (who decides spending, hiring, use of assets) and **related-party transactions** between trusts and family-controlled companies (e.g. a trust buying products from Sri Sri Tattva, leasing land to a family company, paying a family company for services). Nothing on this was found publicly. This is the question that matters for "who benefits".

**Your revenue sense-check:**
- Your estimates: Munich alone 6,000 x EUR 75 + 2,000 x EUR 450 = **about EUR 1.35M**. Europe weekly courses 20 x 8.5 people x EUR 210 = **about EUR 1.9M a year**. Europe plausibly EUR 5 to 10M a year in course revenue before retreats and the German ashram.
- **Why public figures look low:** the Rs 331 crore (about EUR 35M) was money *sent to India*, cumulative over FY2007 to 2016, not total revenue; most course income stays in-country for venues, staff, tours and events. The USD 12.7M covers one US entity; others exist (IAHV US, AOL Health & Education Trust, possibly the Art of Living Retreat Center in North Carolina as a separate entity). Many national entities do not publish accounts (e.g. the German e.V.). Fees paid directly to the Indian ashram for courses are service income, not "foreign contributions".
- **Conclusion:** your estimate does not contradict the filings; they measure different things. Nobody has publicly added up global revenue.
